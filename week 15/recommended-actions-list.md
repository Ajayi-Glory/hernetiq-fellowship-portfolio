
## 1. Regulatory Exposure Assessment

| No. | Governance Area | Potential Compliance Gap | Regulatory Exposure / Consequence | Risk Level | Recommended Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data Protection and Privacy | Personal data may be processed without adequate safeguards or a clearly defined lawful basis. | Data protection violations, investigations, financial penalties, and loss of public trust. | High | Establish lawful processing procedures, data minimisation, and retention controls. |
| 2 | Transparency and Explainability | Users may not be adequately informed about AI involvement in decisions. | Complaints, reduced accountability, and possible transparency violations. | Medium–High | Provide appropriate AI disclosures and understandable explanations. |
| 3 | Accountability and Governance | Roles and responsibilities for AI-related decisions may be unclear. | Weak oversight and difficulty demonstrating accountability. | High | Assign accountable owners and document governance responsibilities. |
| 4 | Human Oversight | Important AI decisions may lack sufficient human review. | Harmful decisions, legal disputes, and potential non-compliance. | High | Introduce human review, escalation, and override mechanisms. |
| 5 | AI Security and Risk Management | Insufficient security testing, risk assessments, and incident response controls. | Data breaches, system manipulation, and operational disruption. | High | Conduct regular security assessments and AI threat modelling. |
| 6 | Fairness and Non-Discrimination | AI outputs may produce biased or discriminatory outcomes. | Discrimination complaints, legal challenges, and reputational damage. | High | Conduct fairness testing and monitor AI outcomes. |
| 7 | Documentation and Auditability | AI development, deployment, and decision records may be incomplete. | Inability to demonstrate compliance or investigate incidents. | Medium–High | Maintain technical documentation, risk registers, and audit logs. |
| 8 | Third-Party Risk | External AI providers may not receive sufficient due diligence. | Third-party security failures, data misuse, and contractual exposure. | Medium–High | Conduct vendor assessments and establish contractual safeguards. |

## 2. Relevant Regulatory and Governance References

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Supports structured identification, measurement, management, and governance of AI risks.

### EU AI Act

Establishes risk-based obligations for AI systems within its scope, including relevant requirements for transparency, oversight, and risk management.

### GDPR

Provides requirements for personal data processing, transparency, accountability, and security where applicable.

### Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023

Provides a framework for protecting personal data and establishing accountability for applicable processing activities.

### ISO/IEC 42001

Provides requirements for establishing and improving an AI management system.

## 3. Risk Classification

| Risk Level | Meaning | Suggested Response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Potential for severe harm or major regulatory exposure. | Immediate attention and remediation. |
| High | Significant potential impact on compliance, security, or operations. | Prioritised corrective action. |
| Medium | Moderate exposure requiring planned improvements. | Address within an agreed remediation period. |
| Low | Limited exposure that should still be monitored. | Routine monitoring and review. |

## 4. Assessment Notes

The risk levels are preliminary qualitative ratings and should be validated using evidence from the organisation's actual systems, policies, and operational environment.

A potential compliance gap is not automatically a confirmed legal violation. Regulatory applicability must be determined based on the organisation's jurisdiction, activities, and relevant legal obligations.
